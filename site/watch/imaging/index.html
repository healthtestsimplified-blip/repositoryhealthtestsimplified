X-ray vs CT scan vs MRI: difference & when

How X-ray, CT and MRI work, how long they take, radiation compared with natural background, safety before MRI (pacemaker, implants, metal), pregnancy, and when doctors usually recommend each scan.

X-ray
Low-dose X-rays make a quick 2D picture in seconds; bone looks white. Chest X-ray ~0.1 mSv, about 10 days of natural background radiation. Used for fractures, chest infection/pneumonia/suspected TB, dental checks, joint arthritis; often the first test.
CT scan
X-rays from all around the body; a computer builds cross-section slices in a few minutes. More radiation: CT head ~1.6 mSv (~7 months of background), CT abdomen ~7.7 mSv (~2.6 years). Used in emergencies: head injury, stroke (to check for bleeding), internal injury, sudden severe belly pain, kidney stone. Contrast sometimes: tell about allergy or kidney disease.
MRI
Strong magnet and radio waves, no X-ray radiation; very fine detail of soft tissue; usually 20–40 minutes, loud. Used for brain and spinal cord problems, slip disc, knee/shoulder ligament injury, nerve problems. Tell beforehand about pacemaker, implants, metal in the body, or fear of closed spaces.
Pregnancy & choice
Tell before any scan if you are or may be pregnant; if imaging is needed, doctors often prefer MRI. The doctor chooses the scan by symptoms, urgency and safety. Avoid unnecessary repeat scans.
