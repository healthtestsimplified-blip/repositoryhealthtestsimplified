Genetics Series Ep 9: Whole genome sequencing: what it adds

For doctors: what whole genome sequencing adds beyond the exome. What it reads, even coverage, CNVs and structural variants in one test, non-coding and mitochondrial DNA, the evidence in children, when to consider it, limits, trio and consent, samples, and a published Indian family case. Case study PDF in the Genetics Clinical Series section.

What it adds
Reads about 3 billion bases (coding and non-coding) versus 1-2% for exome. No capture step, so coverage is more even. Small variants and copy number changes in one test; structural variants, deep intronic and regulatory changes, mitochondrial DNA, some repeat expansions with special analysis.
Evidence & use
Meta-analysis of 39 paediatric studies: 38.6% genome vs 37.8% exome, genome favoured on direct comparison; usual care 7.8% (Nurchis 2023). Consider when exome is negative but suspicion is strong, for suspected structural variants, as one test instead of CMA plus exome, or rapid genome in critically ill newborns (some centres).
Limits
Higher cost and large data; non-coding variants are hard to interpret (more VUS); short-read genome can miss methylation disorders, low-level mosaicism and difficult regions; long-read sequencing is closing gaps. Trio, consent on secondary findings, and reanalysis.
Case study
Indian family with X-linked agammaglobulinemia: exome in four boys found no matching variant; genome found a 5,296 bp deletion removing BTK exons 3-5, absent from global and Indian (IndiGen) databases and confirmed by MLPA. Seven carrier women identified; two boys reported disease-free after sibling transplant (Jain et al., PLoS ONE 2021).
