Genetics Series Ep 1: Which genetic test, when?

For doctors: karyotype vs microarray vs gene panel vs exome, trio testing, reading the report, and a published Indian case (PGIMS Rohtak) where exome found PRRT2 self-limited infantile epilepsy. Case study PDF in the Genetics Clinical Series section.

Levels
Whole-chromosome changes, copy number variants (deletions/duplications), single-gene changes, and special changes such as repeat expansions. Each test sees only some of these.
Karyotype & CMA
Karyotype: chromosome number and balanced translocations; often misses 5–10 Mb changes; still used for obvious chromosomal syndromes and recurrent miscarriage. CMA: first-tier for unexplained DD/ID, autism, multiple anomalies (2010 consensus); yield ~15–20% vs ~3% for karyotype; misses balanced changes.
Panel, exome & special tests
Panel when features point to one disease group (epilepsy: 25+ genes is a first-tier option). Exome: ~20,000 genes, ~36% yield in neurodevelopmental disorders; misses repeat expansions, mitochondrial DNA, many large CNVs. Fragile X: PCR; SMA/deletions: MLPA; imprinting: methylation.
Trio & report
Trio testing helps find de novo changes. Reports use five categories (ACMG/AMP); no treatment decisions on a VUS.
Case study
6-month-old boy with seizures, normal MRI/EEG/metabolic screen, recurrence on phenytoin; exome found a PRRT2 frameshift variant: self-limited infantile epilepsy (Kadian et al., J Family Med Prim Care 2025). Seizures usually stop by about 2 years with normal development (GeneReviews).
