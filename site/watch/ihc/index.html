IHC markers (immunohistochemistry)

Report says 'IHC advised'? What IHC markers are, how the brown stain works, ER/PR and HER2 in breast cancer, and how to read the report.

What it is
Immunohistochemistry: lab-made antibodies stick only to their matching protein in the tissue; a colour reaction turns those cells brown (positive). It shows which protein is present, so the pathologist knows what type of cell it really is.
Sample
Usually no new sample: IHC is done on the same biopsy wax block. If the biopsy was done elsewhere, the block or slides are requested from there.
Right diagnosis
Examples: TTF-1 (lung cancer), S100 (melanoma), CD20 and CD3 (B-cell vs T-cell lymphoma). The pathologist chooses the markers.
Guiding treatment
Breast cancer: ER/PR (less than 1% negative, 1–10% low positive, more than 10% positive; above 1% hormone therapy may be an option) and HER2 (0 to 3+; 3+ positive, 2+ may need FISH, 1+ or 2+ with negative FISH = HER2-low). Source: American Cancer Society.
Report
Each marker is reported positive or negative, sometimes with % and intensity. IHC can take a few extra days. No single marker decides the diagnosis; your doctor decides treatment.
