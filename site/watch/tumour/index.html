Tumour markers (PSA, CA-125, CEA, CA 19-9, AFP)

What tumour markers are, their main use in monitoring after a cancer diagnosis, PSA, CA-125, CEA, CA 19-9 and AFP, why they can rise without cancer, and why a report alone is not a diagnosis.

What & main use
Proteins made by some cancer cells or by the body in response to cancer, measured in blood. Main use: monitoring treatment and recurrence after diagnosis; the trend matters more than one result. Screening with marker panels is not helpful (NHS Scotland/IBMS guidance).
PSA & CA-125
PSA (prostate) can also rise with enlarged prostate, urine infection, prostatitis, catheter; take the sample before a rectal exam. CA-125 (ovary) can also rise with periods, pregnancy, endometriosis, liver or kidney disease.
CEA, CA 19-9, AFP
CEA: bowel cancer monitoring; also rises with smoking and inflammation. CA 19-9: pancreas and bile duct; also rises in jaundice and pancreatitis; some people cannot make it. AFP: liver and some testis/ovary tumours; also high in hepatitis, cirrhosis, pregnancy.
Remember
A normal report does not fully rule out cancer; a high report is not a diagnosis. Diagnosis needs scans and biopsy. Do follow-up tests in the same lab.
