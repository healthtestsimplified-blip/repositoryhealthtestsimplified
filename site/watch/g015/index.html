Genetics Series Ep 15: Fragile X syndrome (field guide)

A field guide for sales and healthcare teams on Fragile X syndrome: FMR1 CGG repeat categories, who is affected, which doctors to visit, Indian data including a Kolkata study, premutation carriers and family testing, which test, who to test, how to talk to doctors, samples and field rules. Bengali narration; Hindi teaser. PDF in the Genetics Clinical Series section.

The gene
FMR1 CGG repeats: normal up to 44; intermediate 45-54; premutation 55-200; full mutation over 200, which switches the gene off. About 1 in 4,000 to 6,250 males; females about half as often, usually milder (UW Lab Medicine test guide).
Indian data
Kolkata (Manovikas Kendra outpatients): 5 of 157 neurobehavioural patients, 3.18% (Bhowmik et al., J Clin Diagn Res 2009). Tamil Nadu: 3 of 29 boys with intellectual disability (Adv Genet 2021). Rural West Bengal school screen: 0 of 179 (IJMR 2009).
Carriers
Premutation: early menopause before 40 in 12-28% of women; tremor-ataxia in about 40% of men and 16-20% of women, usually from age 60-65. One child's result leads to testing for the mother and relatives through a genetic counsellor.
Field approach
Fragile X mutation analysis by PCR; large expansions confirmed by Southern blot or methylation testing. Exome and microarray can miss repeat expansions. Ask: when the exome is negative, do you also test Fragile X? Never blame the mother.
