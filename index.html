Liver Function Test (LFT)

Dr. Health fights Fatty Liver, Hepatitis and Alcohol with the LFT blood test.

Test details
What it checks
SGPT and SGOT (liver damage alarm), bilirubin (jaundice), albumin (liver strength), ALP (bile flow).
Who should test
People who drink alcohol regularly, are overweight or diabetic, take long-term medicines, or have had jaundice or hepatitis.
Approximate normal
SGPT/SGOT up to about 40 U/L · Bilirubin 0.3–1.2 mg/dL · Albumin 3.5–5.0 g/dL
Good to know
Early fatty liver can often be reversed with diet and exercise. Your liver can regrow itself.
Health testsHindi2:16 min

Lipid Profile

Your heart against Bad Cholesterol, Smoking and Junk Food. High cholesterol has no symptoms.

Test details
What it checks
Total cholesterol, LDL (bad), HDL (good) and triglycerides.
Who should test
Every adult from age 20, yearly after 40; people with diabetes, high BP, smoking, extra weight, or early heart attack in the family.
Preparation
Fast for 10–12 hours. Water is fine. Follow your lab's instructions.
Healthy levels (mg/dL)
Total below 200 · LDL below 100 · Triglycerides below 150 · HDL above 40 (men) / 50 (women)

CBC (Complete Blood Count)

The blood team of red cells, white cells and platelets fights Anaemia, Infection and Dengue.

Test details
What it checks
Haemoglobin and RBC (anaemia), WBC (infection), platelets (dengue and bleeding).
Who should test
Tiredness or pale skin, fever for 2 days or more, pregnancy, and a yearly check-up.
Preparation
No fasting needed.
Approximate normal
Hb men 13–17 / women 12–15 g/dL · WBC 4,000–11,000 /cumm · Platelets 1.5–4.5 lakh /cumm

Kidney Function Test (KFT)

Kidneys can fail silently. The kidney hero takes on Diabetes, High BP and painkiller overuse.

Test details
What it checks
Creatinine (filter alarm), urea (waste level), eGFR (filter speed), uric acid and salts.
Who should test
Diabetes or high BP, long-term painkiller use, swelling in feet or face, foamy urine, kidney disease in the family.
Approximate normal
Creatinine men 0.7–1.3 / women 0.6–1.1 mg/dL · Urea 15–40 mg/dL · eGFR 90 or above
Good to know
Usually no fasting needed. Never take painkillers without a doctor's advice.
Health testsHindi2:32 min

Whole Abdomen USG

An ultrasound uses sound waves to find Gallstones, Fatty Liver and Kidney Stones. No radiation, no pain.

Test details
What it shows
Liver and gallbladder, kidneys and bladder, pancreas and spleen, uterus and ovaries or prostate.
Preparation
Fast 6–8 hours. Drink 4–5 glasses of water an hour before and hold urine so the bladder stays full.
Report words
Calculus = stone · Cholelithiasis = gallbladder stones · Hepatomegaly = enlarged liver · Simple cyst = fluid bubble, often harmless
Good to know
Painless, safe in pregnancy, and usually takes 15–20 minutes.
Health testsHindi2:37 min

2D Echo (Echocardiography)

An ultrasound of the heart that checks its size, pumping power and valves.

Test details
What it shows
Heart size and wall thickness, pumping power (EF), valves (open, close or leak), holes and fluid around the heart.
When it's advised
Breathlessness, chest pain, swelling in the feet, a heart murmur, or long-standing high BP or diabetes.
Report words
EF 55–70% = normal pumping · LVH = thick heart wall · MR/TR/AR = leaky valve · Stenosis = narrow valve
ECG vs Echo
ECG checks the heart's electrical rhythm. Echo shows moving pictures of its structure.

Urine Routine (R/E)

A small sample with a big report card, plus the urine colour chart.

Test details
What it checks
Colour and clarity, sugar and protein, pus cells and bacteria (infection), blood and crystals (stone clues).
How to give the sample
First morning urine, clean the area, skip the first few drops, use the lab's sterile cup, and deliver within an hour.
Usually normal
Sugar nil · Protein nil or trace · Pus cells 0–5 /hpf · RBC 0–2 /hpf
Colour guide
Pale yellow = well hydrated · Dark yellow = drink more water · Red or brown = see a doctor

Urine Culture & Sensitivity

Finds the exact germ behind a UTI and which antibiotic will actually work.

Test details
What it does
Grows germs from urine on a lab plate for 48–72 hours, then tests antibiotics against them.
When it's advised
Repeated UTI, no improvement with medicine, fever with burning urine, pregnancy, children and catheter patients.
Report words
No growth = usually no infection · More than 1 lakh CFU/mL = significant · S = antibiotic works · R = it won't
Most important tip
Give the sample before starting antibiotics. Never take antibiotics without a doctor.

HbA1c & Blood Sugar

Why your doctor asks for HbA1c even when the sugar test is normal.

Test details
What it checks
Your average blood sugar over the last 2–3 months.
Who should test
Everyone above 30 yearly; family history of diabetes, extra weight, high BP or PCOS; diabetics every 3 months.
Results
HbA1c below 5.7% normal · 5.7–6.4% prediabetes · 6.5% or above diabetes
Good to know
No fasting needed for HbA1c. Anaemia or thalassaemia can make it misleading.

Vitamin D & B12

Always tired, aching or tingling? It could be low Vitamin D or B12.

Test details
What it checks
Vitamin D for bones and muscles; Vitamin B12 for blood and nerves.
Who should test
People mostly indoors, women over 40 and the elderly, vegetarians, and long-term metformin or acidity-tablet users.
Approximate normal
Vitamin D 30–100 ng/mL · B12 above 300 pg/mL
Good to know
Don't take high-dose Vitamin D on your own. Test first.

Pregnancy care

A three-part series for mothers-to-be.

Pregnancy careHindi2:11 min

Double Marker Test

Part 1. A first-trimester blood test, and what 'high risk' really means.

Test details
What it is
A blood test (beta-hCG and PAPP-A) read together with the NT scan.
When
Between 11 and 14 weeks of pregnancy. If missed, triple or quad marker at 15–20 weeks.
What it screens for
The chance of Down syndrome (21), Edwards (18) and Patau (13).
Remember
Screening is not diagnosis. Most high-risk results still have healthy babies. Baby's sex is never disclosed.
Pregnancy careHindi2:26 min

NIPT

Part 2. Reads tiny pieces of the baby's DNA in the mother's blood.

Test details
What it is
Non-Invasive Prenatal Test: one blood sample from the mother. Safe for the baby.
When
From 10 weeks of pregnancy. Results usually in 1–2 weeks.
Accuracy
Detects over 99% of Down syndrome cases, but it is still a screening test.
Good to know
An anomaly scan is still needed. Costlier than double marker. Tell your doctor about twins or IVF.
Pregnancy careHindi2:29 min

CVS & Amniocentesis

Part 3. Diagnostic tests that give a confirmed answer.

Test details
CVS
At 11–14 weeks. A tiny sample of placenta, taken under ultrasound.
Amniocentesis
From 15 weeks. 3–4 teaspoons of the fluid around the baby.
What they confirm
Chromosome conditions, thalassaemia major in the baby, and other genetic diseases.
Safety
Done by an expert in a few minutes. Small miscarriage risk, usually under 1%. Rh-negative mothers may need anti-D.

Genetic tests

Karyotype, microarray and exome: finding the cause in the genes.

Genetic testsHindi2:42 min

Karyotyping

Counts all 46 chromosomes. Key test for repeated miscarriages and infertility.

Test details
What it checks
All 46 chromosomes (23 pairs): extra or missing chromosomes and swapped pieces (translocations).
Who should test
Couples with 2 or more miscarriages, infertility or very low sperm count, a child with delay or birth defects, girls with short height and no periods, some blood cancers (bone marrow).
Sample & time
Usually a blood sample; in pregnancy, a CVS or amniocentesis sample. Report in about 2–3 weeks.
Reading the report
46,XX = normal female · 46,XY = normal male · 47,XY,+21 = Down syndrome. The first number is the total count.
Good to know
Very small changes are missed; microarray or exome may be needed. Baby's sex is not disclosed in pregnancy.
Genetic testsHindi2:51 min

Chromosomal Microarray: 750K vs 315K

Finds tiny missing or extra chromosome pieces that a karyotype cannot see.

Test details
What it checks
Small deletions (missing pieces) and duplications (extra pieces) across all chromosomes.
750K vs 315K
750K: about 7.5 lakh probes, finer detail, mostly for children and adults. 315K: about 3.15 lakh probes, focused on key regions, mostly for pregnancy (CVS/amnio) and miscarriage tissue.
Who should test
Developmental delay, autism, birth defects, a problem seen on the baby's scan, repeated miscarriage or stillbirth.
Why it matters
Finds a cause in about 15–20% of children with delay or autism, versus about 3–5% with karyotype.
Good to know
Does not show balanced translocations (karyotype) or single-letter changes (exome). Parents' samples may be needed.
Genetic testsHindi2:40 min

Whole Exome Sequencing

Reads about 20,000 genes letter by letter when other tests give no answer.

Test details
What it checks
The exome: the 1–2% of DNA that holds the recipes for proteins, where most known disease-causing changes are found.
Who should test
Delayed development, unexplained fits or muscle weakness, multiple birth defects, unknown genetic illness in the family, or when other tests gave no answer.
Sample & time
Blood (or saliva). Report in about 3–6 weeks. Testing the child with both parents ('trio') gives more reliable results.
Reading the report
Positive (cause found) · Negative (no cause found, genetic not ruled out) · VUS (change of uncertain meaning). About 1 in 3 families find an answer.
Good to know
Cannot catch every change (e.g. Fragile X). Genetic counselling before and after is important.

What is a Cell?

You are made of about 30 lakh crore cells. Each one is a tiny factory.

Test details
Parts
Membrane = security wall · Nucleus = head office · Mitochondria = powerhouse · Ribosomes = protein workers
Lifespan
Skin cells 2–4 weeks · Red blood cells about 120 days · Many nerve cells a whole lifetime
Lab link
CBC counts blood cells; Pap smear and FNAC look at cells under the microscope.

What is the Nucleus?

Every cell has a boss, except red blood cells.

Test details
Inside
A wall with gates (nuclear pores), DNA files, and the nucleolus.
Fun fact
Red blood cells have no nucleus, which leaves more room for haemoglobin.
Lab link
WBC types are told apart by nucleus shape (the CBC differential).

What is a Chromosome?

46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, and the truth about who decides a baby's sex.

Test details
Basics
46 chromosomes in every cell: 23 from mother, 23 from father.
Myth busted
The father's X or Y decides whether a baby is a boy or girl, not the mother. Sex determination before birth is illegal in India.
Tests
Karyotyping; double marker or NIPT in pregnancy, as your doctor advises.

What is DNA?

A 4-letter code, about 3 billion letters long, in every cell.

Test details
The code
Four letters: A pairs with T, G pairs with C.
Fun fact
You share about 99.9% of your DNA with every human. DNA from one cell is about 2 metres long.
Tests
Genetic disease tests like thalassaemia, some cancer risk genes, paternity, and forensics.

What is a Gene?

DNA is the cookbook, a gene is one recipe, a protein is the dish.

Test details
Basics
About 20,000 genes, each in two copies: one from mother, one from father.
Carriers
Two healthy thalassaemia carriers have a 1 in 4 chance, each pregnancy, of a child with thalassaemia major.
Lifestyle
Family history raises risk, but healthy habits can change the story.

What are Histones?

Protein spools that pack 2 metres of DNA into a tiny cell and switch genes on and off.

Test details
Basics
Eight histones make one core. DNA wraps around it to form a nucleosome, like beads on a string.
Gene switch
Tight wrap = gene off; loose wrap = gene on. This is epigenetics.
Don't confuse
Histones are not antihistamines. Antihistamines are allergy medicines.

Thyroid test (TSH, T3, T4)

Wednesday tips, 7 Oct 2026. Always tired, weight going up, hair falling? Check your thyroid.

Test details
What it checks
TSH (the signal from the brain) and T3, T4 (the thyroid hormones). TSH high usually means a slow thyroid; TSH low usually means a fast thyroid.
Symptoms
Slow (hypo): tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold, hair fall, constipation. Fast (hyper): weight loss, fast heartbeat, sweating, shaky hands, anxiety.
Who should test
Women over 35, before or during pregnancy, after delivery, thyroid in the family, diabetes, PCOD or irregular periods. For newborns, ask your doctor about thyroid screening.
Preparation
Usually no fasting. Morning sample is best. On a thyroid tablet, give the sample before the dose as your doctor says. Tell your doctor if you take biotin.
Good to know
About 1 in 10 adults in India has hypothyroidism. Never stop or change your thyroid tablet on your own.
Daily health tipsHindi1:49 min

Dengue, Malaria or Typhoid?

Monday tips, 5 Oct 2026. Same fever, different test.

Test details
Dengue
High fever, eye and body pain, rash. Test: NS1 (days 1–5), IgM (after day 5), CBC for platelets.
Malaria
Fever with chills that comes and goes. Test: rapid antigen test or blood smear.
Typhoid
Slowly rising fever, stomach upset. Test: blood culture as your doctor advises.
Fever rules
Test within 1–3 days, no antibiotics or painkillers on your own, plenty of water and ORS.
Daily health tipsHindi2:24 min

Thalassaemia: test before marriage

Tuesday tips, 6 Oct 2026. One blood test can protect your future child.

Test details
Carrier vs major
Carriers are usually healthy. Thalassaemia major needs blood transfusions every 2–4 weeks for life.
The risk
If both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1 in 4 chance of thalassaemia major.
The test
CBC gives the first clue; HPLC confirms. Once in a lifetime, ideally before marriage or pregnancy.
Myth
Not all anaemia needs iron. Take iron only as your doctor advises.
